Q1: What are the criteria for promotion to the next academic year?
EXPECTED: At least 50% of the total credits of the ensuing academic year and 90% of the total credits of the previous year, excluding the credits of the ensuing year
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p7 #16', 'criteria_for_passing_exams.pdf p1 #1', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p8 #17']
ANSWER: A student will be promoted to the next academic year only if such student has obtained at least:

a) 50% (accurate up to two decimal digits) of the total credits of the ensuing academic year from which the promotion to the next academic year is being sought, and

b) 90% (accurate up to two decimal digits and rounding off thereafter to full digits) of the total credits of the previous year excluding the credits of the ensuing academic year from which the promotion to the next academic year is being sought.

Sources:
- conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf, page 7

Q2: What is the punishment for carrying a mobile phone into the exam?
EXPECTED: Possession only: cancellation of the entire examination. If the phone was used or help was given: cancellation of the entire examination taken during the year plus debarment for 12 months
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p2 #4', 'regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p5-8 #table_unfair_means_table_2_0', 'regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p5-8 #table_unfair_means_table_1_0']
ANSWER: Based on the provided documents, the punishment for a candidate found in possession of a mobile phone depends on whether they used the material:

- Case 1: If the candidate is found in possession of a mobile phone (along with papers, books, notes, or written notes on question paper, clothes, body, table, desk, ruler, or instruments, which could be helpful in answering the paper) but has not attempted to take any assistance herself or give assistance to any other candidate from such material, the punishment is the cancellation of the entire examination.
Source: regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf, page 5-8

- Case 2: If the candidate is found in possession of a mobile phone (along with papers, books, notes, or written notes on question paper, clothes, body, table, desk, foot rule, or instruments, which could be helpful in answering the paper) and has made actual use of the material herself or given assistance thereof to any other candidate during the examination, the punishment is the cancellation of the entire examination taken by the candidate during the year and further debarring him from appearing at any examination of the university within a span of one year (12 months).
Source: regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf, page 5-8

Q7: Are there any relaxations in minimum attendance criteria under special circumstances?
EXPECTED: The Dean/HOD can condone shortage up to 10%, and under exceptional circumstances the Vice-Chancellor can relax it by up to a further 5%
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p3 #5', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p8 #18', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p4 #6']
ANSWER: Yes, there are relaxations in the minimum attendance criteria under certain circumstances, which are as follows:

- The Dean of Faculty, Dean (Academic Affairs), or Head of the Department may condone an attendance shortage of up to 10% for an individual student for reasons to be recorded in writing.
- Under exceptional circumstances, the Vice-Chancellor may further relax the minimum attendance up to 5%.
- To be considered for this relaxation, the student must produce documents showing that they were busy in authorized activities or suffering from any disease or disorder. The student must submit these documents within seven days of resuming their studies; documents submitted thereafter shall not be considered. 
- Under no circumstances shall any relaxation in attendance beyond these provisions be allowed in any case, and a student who has an aggregate attendance of less than 60% in a semester shall under no circumstances be allowed to appear in the end-term examinations.

Source: conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf, page: 3

Q8: What is the formula for calculating Cummulative Performance Index?
EXPECTED: CPI = sum of (credits × marks) divided by sum of credits, over the courses passed with at least 50%
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p9 #manual_0', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p9 #23', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p9 #22']
ANSWER: The formula for calculating the Cumulative Performance Index (CPI) is:
CPI = (sum of Cn x Mn) divided by (sum of Cn), where the sums are taken only over the courses that the student has passed with not less than 50% marks and for which credits are earned. Cn is the number of credits earned for course n in any semester. Mn is the marks obtained by the student in course n. N is the total number of courses over which the performance is being measured, and n runs from 1 to N. All courses have maximum marks of 100 irrespective of the number of credits assigned to the course. Courses in which the student appeared but secured less than 50% marks are not counted; they are treated as Audit Courses and shown that way in the final mark sheet.

Source: conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf, page 9

EXPECTED: Reimbursement of the registration fee only, for Web of Science/Scopus indexed conferences, up to Rs 10,000 per conference, for one student author who presents the paper.

ANSWER: Yes, you can get your conference registration fee reimbursed, subject to the following conditions, limits, and exceptions:
