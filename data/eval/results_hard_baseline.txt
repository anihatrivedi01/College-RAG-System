Q1: How many times can a student take an academic break?
EXPECTED: Only two academic breaks are allowed for the whole programme. If a further break would be needed (because of non-promotion or detention), the student's admission is cancelled.

ANSWER: Only two academic breaks are permissible for a student for the completion of the academic programme/course. In no situation will a student be allowed to take more than two academic breaks, for any reason whatsoever, including for the reasons of detention for shortage of attendance or deficiency of credits during the whole term of completion of the course/programme. 

If a student has exhausted two academic breaks and a further occasion arises for her to take an academic break because of non promotion or detention, the admission of such student would automatically stand cancelled right at the time such an occasion of more than two academic breaks arise.

ANSWER: Based on the provided documents, the differences in placement are as follows:

Q8: CPI formula
EXPECTED: CPI = sum of (credits × marks) divided by sum of credits, over the courses passed with at least 50%
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p9 #manual_0', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p9 #23', 'conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf p8 #20']
ANSWER: Formula for calculating the Cumulative Performance Index (CPI), clause 12(1): CPI = (sum of Cn x Mn) divided by (sum of Cn), where the sums are taken only over the courses that the student has passed with not less than 50% marks and for which credits are earned. Cn is the number of credits earned for course n in any semester. Mn is the marks obtained by the student in course n. N is the total number of courses over which the performance is being measured, and n runs from 1 to N. All courses have maximum marks of 100 irrespective of the number of credits assigned to the course. Courses in which the student appeared but secured less than 50% marks are not counted; they are treated as Audit Courses and shown that way in the final mark sheet.

Source: conduct_and_evaluation_of_examinations.pdf, page 9

Q9: What is the difference between the penalty for possessing notes and using them ?
EXPECTED: Possessing notes without using them or helping anyone: cancellation of the entire examination. Using the material or helping another candidate: cancellation of the entire examination taken during the year, plus debarment from any University examination for 12 months
RETRIEVAL HIT: True   RETRIEVED: ['regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p5-8 #table_unfair_means_table_1_0', 'regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p5-8 #table_unfair_means_table_2_0', 'regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf p5-8 #table_unfair_means_table_5_2']
ANSWER: Based on the provided documents, the penalties differ depending on whether the candidate merely possessed the material or actually used it:

1. Case 1 (Possession without use): If a candidate is found in possession of a mobile phone, papers, books, notes, or written notes on a question paper, clothes, body, table, desk, ruler, or instruments that could be helpful in answering the paper, but has not attempted to take assistance herself or give assistance to any other candidate from such material, the punishment is the cancellation of the entire examination.
Source: regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf, page: 5-8

2. Case 2 (Possession with actual use): If a candidate is found in possession of a mobile phone, papers, books, notes, or written notes on a question paper, clothes, body, table, desk, foot rule, or instruments that could be helpful in answering the paper, and has made actual use of the material herself or given assistance thereof to any other candidate during the examination, the punishment is the cancellation of the entire examination taken by the candidate during the year, and further debarring the candidate from appearing at any examination of the University within a span of one year (12 months).
Source: regulation_for_unfair_means.pdf, page: 5-8

Q10: I will be writing my exam with a scribe. How much extra time do I get, and what attendance do I need to be allowed to sit the exam?

Attendance: minimum 75% in aggregate across all courses in a semester (the Dean/HOD can condone a shortage of up to 10%, and the Vice-Chancellor can relax it by a further 5% in exceptional circumstances); below 60% aggregate a student cannot appear in the end-term examinations. The documents do not state a different attendance rule for students using a scribe.
